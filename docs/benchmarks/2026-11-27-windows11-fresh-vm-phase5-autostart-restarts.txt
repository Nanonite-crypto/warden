The restarts of the virtual machine while the autostart kit ran on
2026-11-27, and what each of them took.

The kit asks for Windows to be restarted twice. The first time this was
done, the restart never finished, and the machine was reset by hand. The
kit was then run to its end, and through a second time, and after that
the machine was restarted three more times with nothing done before. This
file says what was seen and measured of all nine restarts.

Where the numbers come from
---------------------------

- "Windows' shutdown" and "next start" are events 200 and 100 of the
  guest's log Microsoft-Windows-Diagnostics-Performance/Operational: the
  time the event gives ("Incident Time (UTC)") and the duration it gives.
  A service that Windows names as having held the shutdown up is its
  event 203. These were read off the guest's Event Viewer on the screen,
  by hand. They are not in the serial capture.
- The times are the guest's clock. It was about nine hours ahead of the
  real time, so restart A is at 17:34 by the guest and was at 08:34 UTC,
  and the others are on 2026-11-28 by the guest and were between 20:07
  and 20:39 UTC on 2026-11-27.
- "Back at the desktop" is what the VirtualBox window showed, by the
  host's clock, to about a quarter of a minute.
- Restarts F to I were not looked up in the guest's log. That each of
  them happened shows in the serial capture, where the machine's firmware
  writes 87 bytes at every start. The capture has ten times that: the
  start in the morning, the start after the reset by hand, and one for
  each of the restarts B to I.
- VirtualBox's own log of the machine was read on the screen as well.

Restart A

- Windows was told to restart at 09:34 local time (08:34 UTC). The screen
  went black and stayed black.
- VirtualBox's log has "xHCI: USB Suspended" and "HDA: Reset" at 00:06:27
  of the machine's running time, which is about when the restart was
  asked for. Four minutes later those were still its last lines. At 21:02
  local time its last lines were about the host's sound devices, and a
  search for "Hardware reset" found two lines, the later one at 00:00:12
  of the running time, from the start in the morning. Nothing in the log
  spoke of a second start.
- Moving the mouse over the window changed nothing, and neither did a key
  sent to it.
- At 21:03 local time, after 11 hours and 29 minutes, the machine was
  reset by hand with VirtualBox's "Reset". Windows started and was at the
  desktop within 45 seconds.
- Windows' own record of that shutdown, written at this next start, says
  that it took 7,791 ms, and counts it as slower than usual
  ("IsDegradation: true"). It names the Task Scheduler service as having
  held it up: a total time of 4,972 ms, of which 972 ms count as delay.

Restarts B and C

- Windows counts both shutdowns as slower than usual too. For B it names
  no service. For C it names the Task Scheduler service again: a total
  time of 4,977 ms, of which 977 ms count as delay. Both restarts were
  quick.

Restart D

- The screen was black 45 seconds after the restart was asked for, showed
  the mark of a starting Windows after about a minute and a half, and the
  desktop after about two minutes.
- Windows counts neither this shutdown nor that of restart E as slower
  than usual.

What the numbers say
--------------------

- Windows shut down in 7.8 to 10.7 seconds every time it was measured,
  also in the restart that never finished and in the slow one.
- In the three quick restarts that were looked up (B, C, E), the next
  start is stamped 22 to 31 seconds before the end of the shutdown that
  came before it. The stamps on the two sides of a start are therefore
  not on one clock, and cannot be subtracted as they stand. The guest's
  clock is apparently set anew when Windows starts; that was not looked
  into. The stamps can be compared with one another: in restart D the
  next start is stamped 74 seconds after the end of the shutdown. If the
  clock was set by about as much each time, D took 96 to 105 seconds
  longer than B, C and E between the end of Windows' shutdown and the
  beginning of its next start. The screen agrees as far as it can: the
  desktop was there after about two minutes where it was otherwise there
  within three quarters of one.
- That is where restart A stopped as well: Windows had shut down, and the
  machine was not started again.

What is not known
-----------------

- Why. The two restarts that did not go as usual are the two that followed
  the kit's first run, after Warden had removed the entries. The seven
  others went as usual: two after Warden had put the entries back, two
  after the kit had cleaned up with Windows' own tools, three with nothing
  done before. Nine restarts are few, and two of nine can fall on the
  same step by chance.
- What the kit's first run leaves behind that could matter after Windows
  has shut down. Nothing was found. Among the warnings and errors of the
  guest's System log, the shutdown before D has two entries, an error of
  DistributedCOM (10010) and a warning of WinSrvExt (100), and the
  shutdowns before B and E have the same two. Around A that log was not
  looked at.
- Whether it is the virtual machine. No other machine and no other
  hypervisor was tried. This machine has left a black screen twice
  before: at a restart during Windows Setup, before anything of Warden's
  was on it, and once in the middle of a scan (docs/TESTING.md has both,
  docs/BENCHMARKS.md the second).

Not tried: the entries made and removed with Windows' own tools alone,
followed by a restart; the five timed looks alone, followed by a restart;
more restarts of each kind.

The machine: Windows 11 Enterprise Evaluation 26H2 (10.0.26300, build
26300.9457) as a guest of Oracle VirtualBox with 4 processors and 8 GB of
memory, EFI with Secure Boot, a TPM 2.0, and "Nested Paging, Hyper-V
Paravirtualization" as its acceleration. The host is the desktop of the
other results.
